# Work-permit cheat-sheet: Indian citizen, B.Tech (IIT BHU 2023), ~3 yrs experience

Checked 2026-10-15 against official pages where they would load; figures are **approximate, 2026**, and thresholds change every January (or earlier). **Verify every number on the official page before relying on it.** Not legal advice.

Common pattern everywhere: you cannot apply on your own. An employer offers a job first, and in most countries the employer must be registered or approved, so a posting that says "visa sponsorship" is the real filter. A B.Tech (4 years) clears the usual "degree of 3+ years" test; some countries want the degree checked or recognised, so keep your transcript and degree certificate ready.

| Country | Route | Salary floor (approx, 2026) | Employer must be... | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweden | EU Blue Card (degree 180 credits or 5 yrs experience; contract of 6+ months) | SEK 53,625/month from 15 Jul 2026 (1.25x average salary) | Offering a signed contract; no register | 75% of complete applications decided within 1 month, 2 months if incomplete (Migrationsverket) |
| Sweden | General work permit | New rule from 1 Jun 2026: about 90% of Swedish median salary (no SEK figure on the official news page; verify) | Offering the job; employer pays a collective-agreement-level wage | Waiting times page gives no fixed figure |
| Norway | Skilled worker permit (degree or vocational training, job needing it) | About NOK 522,600/year for bachelor-level, NOK 599,200 for master-level from 1 Sep 2025 (secondary sources; UDI's own page showed older, lower numbers, so verify) | Offering the job; collective-agreement pay where one applies | UDI publishes monthly estimates, no fixed figure |
| Denmark | Pay Limit Scheme | DKK 552,000/year (2026); supplementary scheme DKK 446,000 for positive-list roles | A Denmark-registered company that files its part of the application | Normal processing page on nyidanmark.dk; the Fast-track route (certified employers) is much quicker |
| Finland | Specialist permit / EU Blue Card (same salary since 2024) | EUR 3,937 gross/month (2026), fringe benefits excluded | Offering confirmed employment | Specialist fast-track route advertised at about 2 weeks; verify |
| Netherlands | Highly skilled migrant | Age 30+: about EUR 5,942/month; under 30: about EUR 4,357/month (2026, secondary sources). At 27-28 you are likely in the under-30 band | **IND-recognised sponsor**, check the public register | IND must decide within 90 days; recognised sponsors usually faster |
| Germany | EU Blue Card | About EUR 50,700/year; reduced about EUR 45,934/year for IT/shortage roles and recent graduates (2026, secondary sources) | Offering a qualifying job; degree recognition (anabin/ZAB) may be asked; embassy visa step from India | Visa 4 to 12 weeks plus appointment wait, then residence card (estimates vary) |
| Ireland | Critical Skills Employment Permit | EUR 40,904/year (listed occupations, relevant degree) or EUR 36,848 within 12 months of graduating; EUR 68,911 for other eligible occupations (from 1 Mar 2026) | Offering the job; applies 12+ weeks before start | DETE publishes the date it is currently processing, no fixed figure |
| United Kingdom | Skilled Worker visa | Higher of GBP 41,700 or the occupation's going rate; GBP 33,400 option under "paid less" conditions (conditions apply) | **Licensed sponsor** (check the register); job must be at degree-level skill | Roughly 3 weeks from outside the UK (secondary sources conflict) |

## Reading the table for your profile
- Software and AI engineer and product roles at degree level normally qualify in all of these. Check the Irish occupation list and the UK going rate for your exact job title.
- The Netherlands, UK and Denmark (fast-track) lean on employers who are already registered sponsors, so large and venture-backed companies are safer bets than small ones.
- Dutch and German reduced thresholds for younger or recent-graduate applicants may apply to you; confirm on the official page which band your age and graduation date fall in.
- Remote roles that are open "worldwide" avoid permits entirely, but check tax and contractor rules separately.
